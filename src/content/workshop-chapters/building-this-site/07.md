The speaking page's real blocker was strategy, not design: how to headline a day job's title without competing with it. The answer — wear it openly, refer consulting inquiries elsewhere by name every time, and prove "effective speaker" with real clips, a real ticket wall, and a written sample instead of just asserting it — shipped the same session it got resolved, twice: once with placeholder boxes standing in for missing assets, then again with the weakest of those placeholders dropped outright once three empty "pending" boxes read as more unfinished than simply not having them.

The colophon — the one page every provenance stamp and footer link on the site had pointed at for chapters, always 404ing — finally shipped. Every placeholder link in its own mockup got replaced with something real, including a worked example that the mockup had written as a hypothetical before the thing it referenced actually existed.

The last three dead links in the footer's "elsewhere" column all got resolved for real: a real podcast link, a real RSS feed — the site's first — and profile links in place of a public email address, which would have been a second, weaker version of a contact surface the site already has properly built elsewhere.

## Main takeaways

- When a placeholder treatment for missing assets reads as more unfinished than the assets simply not being there, drop them.
- A mockup's own hypothetical example can become real between the design pass and the build pass — worth checking rather than assuming it's still hypothetical.
- A public contact address isn't free — it's a second, weaker surface plus standing exposure, when a real one already exists elsewhere on the site.

## What I got wrong

- Missed an entire section on the speaking page's first pass, even after agreeing to ship it as a placeholder — caught later by a separate review pass, not any step along the way.
- Whether the RSS feed should ever carry full post content instead of a short meta line is still open.
