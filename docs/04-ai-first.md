## 1. Premise

Most of this site's content is generated by AI riding along with Zack — drafted, structured, and often committed by bots he directs. The site doesn't hide that; it celebrates it. This is the teardown instinct pointed at authorship: instead of a disclaimer in the footer, the collaboration itself is disassembled, labeled, and put on display.

The positioning claim underneath: anyone can slap "made with AI" on a page. This site's disclosure is *auditable* — the repo is public, the commits carry co-author trailers, and every post declares who typed it. Nobody has to take our word for it.

## 2. The rule that makes it safe

**The voice doc governs the bots, not vice versa.** AI-first describes the workflow — drafting, research, scaffolding, commits. Judgment, taste, positions taken, and accountability are conspicuously human. Zack reads everything that publishes, and everything that publishes is his. This line is what separates "built by my bots" from "content farm," and every mechanism below exists to make the line visible.

## 3. Authorship: provenance, not taxonomy

Every post declares its authorship in front matter. This is a third field, **not a third axis** — pillar and format say what a post is about and what it does; authorship says who typed it. It never drives navigation or sections; it renders as a stamp on the post.

```yaml
authorship: duet   # human | duet | bot — who typed it; Zack answers for all three
```

- `human` — Zack wrote it. Bots may have fetched a link or fixed a typo.
- `duet` — AI drafted under direction; Zack reshaped and edited. The default mode.
- `bot` — agent-generated (changelogs, summaries, data-driven posts); Zack reviewed before publish.

Enforced in `src/content.config.ts` alongside the rest of the schema. Display: a small provenance stamp near the byline, styled like a maker's plate on furniture — workbench aesthetic, not a warning label. Stamp links to the colophon (§5).

## 4. The commit trail is the receipt

Agents commit with `Co-Authored-By` trailers (e.g. `Co-Authored-By: Claude <noreply@anthropic.com>`). Combined with the public repo (docs/03), rung one of the GitHub ladder now teaches two things at once: what a commit history is, *and* what supervised human-AI collaboration actually looks like at the level of individual changes. The disclosure mechanism and the GitHub on-ramp are the same feature.

## 5. The colophon: `/how-this-site-is-made`

A standing page — the operating manual as content:

- Which models and tools ride along, and what each is allowed to do.
- What only Zack does (positions, publish decisions, anything with his name's weight behind it).
- The supervision loop: what review actually catches, with real examples.
- Links to the live instruction files in the repo (`AGENTS.md` — already public). Very few people publish the actual instructions their bots run under; that's the celebration.
- What the stamps (§3) mean.

Slug decided 2026-07-26 (entry 013): `/how-this-site-is-made`, the plain-spoken option over "colophon" — matches the teaching-voice brand (brief §2) and was already the live link in `AuthorshipStamp.astro`.

## 6. The audience angle: supervision, in lawyer terms

For the legal-tech audience this is the payload. Lawyers already have the mental model: a supervised nonlawyer assistant. The framing — "I supervise my bots the way you supervise a paralegal: the judgment is mine, the signature is mine, here's what my review catches" — maps to the conversation the profession is already having (ABA Formal Opinion 512 territory, Model Rule 5.3 instincts). The site becomes a live demonstration of responsible AI use that an attorney can adopt the next morning, exactly the move the GitHub ladder makes for version control.

This framing earns teardown/log content of its own: the collaboration is a standing subject. What the AI got wrong is the "What I got wrong" section, industrialized.

## 7. Costs get an AI line

The build log's cost-honesty rule extends: entries report tokens/dollars of AI spend alongside money and hours, whenever the workflow meaningfully involved the bots. Real numbers about AI-assisted work are rare; that scarcity is the opportunity.

## 9. Open questions

- Do the bots get names/personas, or stay generic ("the bots")? Named agents are more fun and more honest about there being several; also more twee. Defer to the brand pass.
- Does `duet` need splitting (AI-drafted vs. AI-edited)? Start with three values; let real posts argue for more.
- Whether the newsletter carries the stamp too. Leaning yes — email is where trust is most personal.
- Machine-readable provenance beyond front matter (schema.org, C2PA-adjacent conventions) — watch, don't build.
