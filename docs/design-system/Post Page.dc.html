What law firm software actually costs

Six vendors, six pricing models, one spreadsheet — a real accounting of what a case management system costs once you add seats, migration, and the modules they don't mention in the demo.

Every vendor demo starts the same way: a clean dashboard, a friendly rep, and a number that sounds reasonable until you ask what it doesn't include. I asked six vendors the same six questions and built the spreadsheet they were hoping I wouldn't.

The base price is the least of it

Per-seat pricing looks simple until you account for the modules that get bundled in the sales call but billed separately at renewal. Document automation, e-signature, and client portals are routinely quoted as included, then itemized in year two.

"The demo shows you the car. The invoice shows you the car, the extended warranty, and the undercoating you didn't ask for."

Migration is where budgets die

Every firm I talked to underestimated migration cost by at least half. The vendors know this. Ask for a fixed-fee migration quote in writing before you sign anything — not an estimate.

The mix is the point: the same teardown instinct that finds the hidden line-item works on a training plan or a workbench too.

None of this means the software is bad. It means the pricing model is built to be evaluated one feature at a time, and firms that ask for the total cost of ownership up front consistently negotiate better terms.
